\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We probe a two-branch autoencoder (\textbf{DUALVAE}) that combines a discrete
vector-quantized (VQ) branch with a continuous (VAE) branch by \emph{summing} them,
$z = z_{\text{vq}} + z_{\text{cont}}$, before an attention block and the decoder. We test two
claims: (A) the continuous branch does \emph{not} suffer posterior collapse, and (B) the
sum forms a \emph{Gaussian mixture} rather than a single blob. We add two more views: a
DINO-style visualization of the latent features painted back onto the image, and a
branch-ablation of reconstruction quality. We run four checkpoints: \textbf{Run~A}
($\beta{=}0.001$) and \textbf{Run~B} ($\beta{=}0.1$) with the standard prior, and
\textbf{Run~C} ($\beta{=}0.1$) and \textbf{Run~D} ($\beta{=}0.001$) with a \emph{code-centered
prior} that moves the Gaussian prior onto the codeword. The headline: Claim~A holds in all four
runs; Claim~B is weak in Run~A, emerging in Run~B, and in \textbf{Runs~C and D becomes a
statistically confirmed, well-separated Gaussian mixture} (separability $>2$, GMM beats a single
Gaussian on every test, dip test multimodal). The two standard-prior runs reveal a clean
\textbf{rate/separability trade-off}; the code-centered prior \textbf{breaks} it --- the mixture
comes from the prior \emph{shape}, not the KL strength, so \textbf{Run~D} pairs a clean mixture
with near-best reconstruction ($0.870$ SSIM, vs Run~A's $0.882$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup in plain words}
The model squeezes an image into a small grid of latent vectors (here an $8$-channel grid
of $32\times32$ locations, $256\times256$ inputs, downsample~$8$). At each location it
produces \emph{two} things and adds them:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$z_{\text{vq}}$ (discrete):} the nearest entries from a learned codebook of
  $256$ ``words'' (with residual depth~$2$). Think of it as picking a \emph{category-like
  anchor} for that patch.
  \item \textbf{$z_{\text{cont}}$ (continuous):} a normally-distributed offset from a VAE
  branch, meant to add the leftover detail.
\end{itemize}
The KL weight $\beta$ controls how hard the continuous branch is pushed toward its prior.
\textbf{Run~A} uses a \emph{weak} $\beta=0.001$ and \textbf{Run~B} a \emph{stronger}
$\beta=0.1$, both with the standard $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ prior. \textbf{Runs~C and D} replace that prior with a \emph{code-centered} one: the continuous branch
encodes a residual $\Delta$ around the code and the prior becomes $\mathcal{N}(e_k,\sigma_k^2)$
(flags \texttt{residual\_continuous}, \texttt{component\_prior}, \texttt{use\_ema\_codebook};
see \texttt{reports/code\_centered\_prior.pdf}). Run~C uses $\beta=0.1$ and Run~D uses
$\beta=0.001$, so the pair isolates the effect of KL strength \emph{under} the new prior. All
numbers below come from $200$ validation images (latent statistics), $500$--$1500$ images
(reconstruction metrics), and $5$ images (feature pictures).

Later experiments add a perceptual objective (LPIPS + GAN; the ``+'' runs), a wavelet detail
branch (the ``++'' runs), and a cross-view code-invariance term (CVI). Table~\ref{tab:ckpts}
maps every run label to its checkpoint directory.

\section{Experiment 1 --- Does the continuous branch collapse?}
\textbf{What ``collapse'' means.} A VAE branch collapses when its output stops depending on
the image: the posterior $q(z\mid x)$ just becomes the prior $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ (variance
$\to 1$, mean $\to 0$), so the latent carries no information and the decoder ignores it.

\textbf{What we measured (and why it is easy to read).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Active units:} count latent dimensions that actually change from image to
  image ($\mathrm{Var}_x(\mathbb{E}[z\mid x])>0.01$). Collapse $\Rightarrow$ near $0$.
  \item \textbf{KL to $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ (nats):} how far the posterior is from the prior.
  Collapse $\Rightarrow$ near $0$.
  \item \textbf{Posterior std / $|$mean$|$:} collapse sits at std$=1$, mean$=0$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experiment 2 --- Is the sum a Gaussian \emph{mixture}?}
\textbf{The idea in one line.} Because $z = e_k + \Delta$ (a codeword plus a Gaussian
offset), the overall distribution is, by construction,
$p(z)=\sum_k \pi_k\,\mathcal{N}(e_k,\Sigma_k)$: a Gaussian mixture whose \emph{centers are
the codewords} and whose \emph{weights are how often each code is used}. The real question
is whether those components are \emph{separated} or just smeared into one blob.

\textbf{What we measured.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Separability ratio} $=$ (typical distance between code centers) $/$
  (continuous spread). $>1$ means the codes stand apart from the noise; $<1$ means the
  continuous offset drowns them.
  \item \textbf{Single-Gaussian vs.\ mixture:} BIC (model selection) and the held-out
  log-likelihood of the \emph{code-anchored} mixture vs.\ a single Gaussian.
  \item \textbf{Cluster quality:} silhouette using the code as the label.
  \item \textbf{Multimodality:} project onto the axis between the two \emph{most-separated}
  code centers; report a 2-mode separation (distance between the two fitted modes in units
  of their width) and Hartigan's dip test.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experiment 3 --- What do the features look like on the image?}
\textbf{What we did (the DINO trick).} For a few images we take each branch's latent grid,
run PCA over the location vectors, and paint the top-3 components as an RGB image (and the
1st component as a heatmap). Similar features get similar colors, so we can literally
\emph{see} what each branch encodes, painted back onto the image.

\textbf{Plain-words result} (Figs.~\ref{fig:featA} and~\ref{fig:featB}). Reconstructions are
nearly identical to the originals in both runs. The \textbf{VQ features} trace object shapes
and layout (the truck, the headphones, the gas pump are visible as coherent colored
regions); the \textbf{continuous features} look more like fine, high-frequency texture; the
\textbf{summed PC1 heatmap} lights up object contours, just like DINO's ``foreground''
component. So the discrete branch carries \emph{structure} and the continuous branch carries
\emph{detail} --- consistent with the ablation below. The effect is visible in both runs;
in Run~B (higher $\beta$) the VQ features carry even more of the object structure, matching
the ablation finding that the codes do more of the reconstruction work there.

\section{Experiment 5 --- What do the codes \emph{really} contribute?}
Experiment~4 shows that deleting a branch hurts, but it has a hidden confound: the decoder
was trained on the \emph{sum} $z_{\text{vq}}+z_{\text{cont}}$, so zeroing a branch removes
information \emph{and} pushes the decoder input off the manifold it ever saw. A direct
measurement makes this concrete: in Run~A most of the discrete signal is a single
\textbf{constant offset} added at every location (shared-DC part $\|\overline{z_{\text{vq}}}\|
= 0.79$, which is $64\%$ of $\|z_{\text{vq}}\|$); in Run~B the codes are mostly
location-specific ($82\%$). So ``zeroing the codes'' partly just deletes a constant the
decoder leans on. We ran four experiments to pull these effects apart.

\paragraph{Exp 5.1 \& 5.2 --- Replace-with-mean and random-code swap (in easy words).}
Keeping the continuous branch fixed, we feed the decoder four versions of the discrete part:
the \emph{real} codes (\texttt{full}); the \emph{dataset-average} code at every location
(\texttt{code\_mean} --- keeps the constant offset, throws away per-image code information);
the real codes \emph{shuffled} across locations (\texttt{code\_random} --- same code
statistics, wrong image); and \emph{zero} (\texttt{code\_zero}, i.e.\ \texttt{vanilla\_only}).
This lets us split the ``codes help'' gap into two named pieces:
\[
\underbrace{Q(\texttt{full})-Q(\texttt{code\_mean})}_{\text{real per-image code INFO}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\underbrace{Q(\texttt{code\_mean})-Q(\texttt{code\_zero})}_{\text{DC / off-manifold effect}} .
\]

\textbf{Plain-words result.} In \textbf{Run~A} about two-thirds of the ``codes help'' gap is
just the decoder missing a constant offset (an \emph{artifact} of zeroing, not information);
only $\sim$one-third is real per-image code content --- visible in the grid, where
\texttt{code\_mean} looks almost like \texttt{full} while \texttt{code\_zero} is
\emph{desaturated}. In \textbf{Run~B} it flips: $\sim$$98\%$ of the gap is genuine per-image
information (dropping the codes' spatial pattern destroys the image), and the constant offset
barely matters. The random-code swap agrees: shuffling the codes costs $+0.06$ SSIM in Run~A
but a huge $+0.59$ in Run~B, i.e.\ putting the \emph{right} code in the \emph{right} place
matters enormously in Run~B and only a little in Run~A.

\paragraph{Exp 5.3 --- Which frequencies each branch carries (Fig.~\ref{fig:spec}).}
We take the pixel-space contribution of each branch ($\Delta$codes $=$
\texttt{full}$-$\texttt{code\_mean}, $\Delta$cont $=$ \texttt{full}$-$\texttt{cont\_zero})
and look at its power across spatial frequencies. In Run~A the continuous branch dominates
\emph{every} frequency (it does all the work). In Run~B the curves cross: the \textbf{codes
carry the low/coarse frequencies} (the bulk of the image) while the \textbf{continuous branch
takes over at high/fine frequencies} (the texture) --- a clean coarse/detail division of
labour.

\section{Experiment 6 --- Moving the prior onto the code (Runs~C and D)}
\emph{Run~C uses the code-centered prior with $\beta=0.1$; Run~D uses the identical recipe with
$\beta=0.001$. Together they show the mixture comes from the prior \emph{shape}, not the KL
strength --- and that low $\beta$ (Run~D) buys back the reconstruction quality.}

\subsection*{Why the standard prior cannot make a mixture}
In Runs~A and B the continuous branch $z_{\text{cont}}=\mu+\sigma\odot\varepsilon$ is
regularized by $\mathrm{KL}\!\big(\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)\big)$, which
pushes $\mu\to 0$ --- toward the \emph{origin}, and \emph{independently of the code}. The code
$z_{\text{vq}}=e_k$ is merely added afterwards. So the continuous branch is free to fill a
full unit-variance Gaussian whose spread ($\sim\!1.0$) dwarfs the codeword spacing
($\sim\!0.1$): the codewords are tiny bumps inside one big cloud and no mixture is visible.
This is not fixed by ``summing the code into the mean before the reparameterization'':
$z_{\text{vq}}+(\mu+\sigma\varepsilon)$ and $(\mu+z_{\text{vq}})+\sigma\varepsilon$ are the
\emph{same} number, so with the same KL nothing changes.

\subsection*{The fix: make the codeword the \emph{center} of the prior}
Write the continuous branch as a \emph{residual} $\Delta$ around the code, $z=e_k+\Delta$ with
$\Delta=\mu_\Delta+\sigma\odot\varepsilon$, and change the prior so that, \emph{given} the
code $k$, the latent is expected to sit \emph{on} the codeword:
\begin{equation}
p(z\mid k)=\mathcal{N}(e_k,\sigma_k^2 I)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p(\Delta\mid k)=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_k^2 I).
\end{equation}
The regularizer becomes a KL between the residual posterior and this code-centered prior,
which has a clean per-dimension form:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KL}\!\big(\mathcal{N}(\mu_\Delta,\sigma^2)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_k^2)\big)
=\log\frac{\sigma_k}{\sigma}+\frac{\sigma^2+\mu_\Delta^2}{2\sigma_k^2}-\tfrac12 .
\label{eq:klc}
\end{equation}
The decisive term is $\mu_\Delta^2/(2\sigma_k^2)$: it penalizes the residual \emph{mean}, so
training now drives $\mu_\Delta\to 0$, i.e.\ it pulls $z$ \emph{toward the codeword $e_k$}
instead of toward the origin. Averaging over how often each code is used ($\pi_k$) gives the
marginal prior
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\,p(z)=\sum_k \pi_k\,\mathcal{N}(e_k,\sigma_k^2 I)\,}
\end{equation}
--- a genuine \textbf{Gaussian mixture} whose component \emph{means are the codewords}, weights
are the code frequencies, and widths are the per-code $\sigma_k$ (estimated online as the EMA
within-code variance $v_k/N_k$). The components are well separated exactly when the within-code
spread is smaller than the gap between codewords,
\begin{equation}
\text{separated}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \sigma_k\ \ll\ \min_{j\neq k}\lVert e_j-e_k\rVert ,
\label{eq:sepc}
\end{equation}
which \eqref{eq:klc} now actively enforces (small residual) while the codes drift apart to carry
information. In the code this is \texttt{residual\_continuous} + \texttt{component\_prior} +
\texttt{use\_ema\_codebook} (full derivation in \texttt{reports/code\_centered\_prior.pdf}).

\textbf{It works --- decisively.} Every Claim-B metric flips to the mixture side (Table~2):
the continuous residual std \emph{drops} from $\approx\!1.0$ to $0.41$ while the codeword
spacing \emph{grows} to $1.12$, so the \textbf{separability ratio jumps to $2.72$} (codes now
stand well apart from the within-code noise, the condition $\sigma_k\ll$ spacing). For the first
time the K-component GMM \textbf{beats} a single Gaussian on BIC ($-10347$), the code-anchored
mixture \textbf{beats} a single Gaussian in held-out likelihood ($+1.27$), the silhouette is
positive ($+0.13$), and \textbf{Hartigan's dip test is significant} ($p=7\times10^{-4}$,
multimodal). The PCA view (Fig.~\ref{fig:gmmC}) shows what the numbers mean: tight, separated
clusters, each sitting on its codeword --- compare the single blob of Run~A.

\textbf{And it is nearly free.} Run~C keeps the continuous branch healthy (no collapse:
$100\%$ active units, KL $1.04$), and its reconstruction ($0.836$ SSIM / $27.85$ PSNR) is
\emph{better than Run~B} ($0.819$ / $27.11$) and close to Run~A --- so we gained a clean,
separated mixture \emph{without} paying the reconstruction cost the plain $\beta$ knob demanded.
The ablation (Table~3) confirms the intended division of labour: the codes now carry almost the
entire image (\texttt{vq\_only} $0.805\approx$ \texttt{full} $0.836$) and the continuous branch
is a small refinement ($+0.03$ SSIM), and the decomposition shows $\approx\!98\%$ of the code
contribution is genuine per-image information (Table, Exp~5).

\subsection*{Run~D: the mixture is free at low $\beta$ too --- and reconstruction returns}
Run~D keeps the code-centered prior but drops $\beta$ from $0.1$ to $0.001$. The mixture
\textbf{survives essentially unchanged}: separability $2.35$, GMM still beats a single Gaussian
on BIC ($-3508$) and held-out likelihood ($+0.63$), silhouette $+0.16$, dip test still
significant ($p=0.005$, multimodal), all $256$ codes used (Fig.~\ref{fig:gmmD}). So the mixture
is produced by the prior's \emph{shape} (the codeword-centered mean), \emph{not} by the strength
of the KL --- exactly what \eqref{eq:klc} predicts, since the KL only shrinks the residual, and
even a weak KL keeps it below the codeword spacing ($0.25 < 0.58$).

\textbf{What the low $\beta$ buys is reconstruction.} With the mixture no longer depending on a
strong KL, the weaker $\beta$ lets the continuous branch run at higher rate (KL $2.57$ nats,
posterior std $0.047$ --- near-deterministic, like Run~A), and reconstruction climbs to
\textbf{$0.870$ SSIM / $28.73$ PSNR}, essentially matching Run~A ($0.882 / 29.09$) and clearly
above Run~C ($0.836 / 27.85$). Run~D therefore \textbf{breaks the rate/separability trade-off
outright}: it is simultaneously (i) a clean, confirmed Gaussian mixture and (ii) an
almost-best-in-class reconstructor, with no collapse ($100\%$ active units) and $\approx\!100\%$
of the code contribution being genuine per-image information (Table, Exp~5). The only price
relative to Run~C is slightly tighter, less widely-spaced components (spacing $0.58$ vs $1.12$),
which cost nothing on any mixture test.

\section{Experiment 7 --- Is the better-organized latent useful downstream?}
A prettier latent is only interesting if it \emph{helps}. So we ask a concrete, honest
question: does the Gaussian-mixture organization make the features more useful for
\textbf{classification} and \textbf{image retrieval}? We test it with the two standard
``frozen-feature'' protocols, \textbf{evaluated on the validation set only}.

\subsection*{What the two protocols mean (in plain words)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Linear probe.} Freeze the model, turn each image into one feature vector, and
  train \emph{only} a simple linear classifier on top to predict the $10$ Imagenette classes.
  If the classes are easy to separate with a straight cut, the features are ``semantically
  organized''. We split the val set in two (stratified): fit the linear classifier on one part,
  report top-$1$ accuracy on the held-out part. Chance $=0.107$.
  \item \textbf{Retrieval mAP.} Use each val image as a query and rank all other val images by
  cosine similarity; a good feature puts same-class images first. We report mean Average
  Precision (mAP) over all queries (leave-one-out). Chance $=0.100$.
\end{itemize}
No part of the network is fine-tuned --- only the frozen features are read out, which is the
honest way to ask ``what did the model already learn?''.

\subsection*{Four features per model}
The latent is an $8$-channel $32\times32$ grid, so we pool it to one vector per image four ways:
\textbf{z\_sum} (mean-pooled full latent, $8$-d), \textbf{z\_vq} (mean-pooled discrete branch,
$8$-d), \textbf{z\_cont} (mean-pooled continuous branch, $8$-d), and \textbf{bagcodes} --- the
normalized histogram of which of the $256$ codewords each image uses ($256$-d, a learned
``bag-of-visual-words''). A matched standalone VAE (mean-pooled $8$-d latent, same LPIPS+GAN recipe)
is a baseline. \emph{Note the VAE here is a GAN model, so its \texttt{z\_sum} is best compared against
the dual \emph{GAN} runs (D+/D+MCM, Exp.~10), not the MSE runs A--D --- the GAN lifts the pooled latent
for both (Exp.~9).}

\subsection*{What we see, and whether it is ``worth it''}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Everything is well above chance but modest.} Probe accuracy $0.24$--$0.37$ vs
  chance $0.107$ ($2$--$3.4\times$); retrieval barely clears chance ($0.10$--$0.15$ vs $0.10$).
  This is the expected ceiling: a \emph{reconstruction} latent captures appearance, not
  semantics, and will not rival a self-supervised model --- as anticipated.
  \item \textbf{The GMM organization genuinely helps the \emph{discrete} representation for
  probing.} \texttt{bagcodes} climbs $0.290\!\to\!0.317\!\to\!0.348\!\to\!0.365$
  (A$\to$D, $+26\%$ relative) and \texttt{z\_vq} climbs $0.240\!\to\!0.306$: as the latent
  becomes a cleaner mixture, the codebook becomes a more class-discriminative vocabulary. This
  is a real, monotonic downstream benefit of the code-centered prior.
  \item \textbf{The continuous branch becomes \emph{less} useful} (\texttt{z\_cont}
  $0.278\!\to\!0.145$): exactly as intended, in the code-prior runs it carries only the small
  within-component detail, so little class signal lives there.
  \item \textbf{The advantage is specifically in the codes, not the pooled latent.} The
  pooled \texttt{z\_sum} \emph{matches the plain VAE at equal recipe}: the matched GAN VAE scores
  \texttt{z\_sum} $0.358$, statistically tied with the dual GAN runs' \texttt{z\_sum} (D+ $0.354$,
  D+MCM $0.355$; Exp.~10) --- the pooled continuous latent carries the same modest class signal with
  or without the codebook. The dual/GMM design only pulls ahead through the discrete
  \texttt{bagcodes}/\texttt{z\_vq} features \emph{the VAE does not have} ($0.44$ vs the VAE's best
  $0.358$).
  \item \textbf{Retrieval does not benefit.} mAP stays $\approx0.13$--$0.15$ across all runs
  (\texttt{bagcodes} best but flat) --- the latent is simply not semantic enough for retrieval.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Verdict.} \emph{Worth it, in a specific and honest sense.} The Gaussian-mixture latent
is not just prettier: it makes the bag-of-codes a measurably better linear-probe descriptor
($+26\%$ over the un-organized Run~A), and Run~D --- already the best reconstructor --- is also
the best on the code features. But the gains are confined to the discrete representation and
to linear probing; retrieval is unmoved and the absolute level confirms a reconstruction latent
is no substitute for self-supervised pre-training. If downstream semantics is the real target,
the codebook is the useful part to build on, and an invariance/prediction objective would be
needed to close the gap to SSL.

code-centered-prior wiring unchanged. We call them \textbf{C+} ($\beta{=}0.1$) and \textbf{D+}

\textbf{LPIPS})? And does the Gaussian mixture \emph{survive} adversarial training?

\section{Experiment 9 --- Making the codes semantic: cross-view code-invariance (D+CVI)}
The downstream probe (Exp.~7) capped around $0.37$ because a \emph{reconstruction} latent encodes
appearance, not semantics. Run~\textbf{D+CVI} tries to fix that with a lightweight auxiliary head,
aiming to \textbf{raise linear-probe/retrieval without sacrificing reconstruction or the mixture}.

\subsection*{What was added for semantics (how it was trained)}
D+CVI is exactly Run~D+ (code-centered prior, LPIPS+GAN, $\beta{=}0.001$) with \emph{one extra
loss term} --- the reconstruction path, decoder, GAN and KL are all unchanged. Per training step:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The clean image is encoded and reconstructed as usual (so \textbf{FID is untouched}).
  \item A second, \emph{appearance-augmented} view of the same image (color jitter, blur,
  grayscale, moderate crop) is encoded (one extra encoder pass, under \texttt{no\_grad} --- cheap).
  \item For both views we form the image-level \textbf{soft bag-of-codes} --- a distribution over
  the $256$ codewords, averaged over locations. The \textbf{cross-view code-invariance (CVI)} loss
  pulls the clean distribution toward the augmented one (cross-entropy, augmented view as a
  stop-grad target): \emph{the codes a patch is assigned to must not change under color/blur/crop.}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{The idea:} linear probing rewards \emph{invariance}, reconstruction rewards
\emph{appearance}, so CVI injects invariance into the \emph{codes} (content) while the continuous
branch keeps absorbing appearance (style) --- a content/style split. Reconstruction still forces
the codes to be informative, so the aux term cannot collapse them; the CVI weight is ramped from
$0$ after the codebook settles. (Implementation in \texttt{experiments/train\_dualvae.py}; design
in \texttt{reports/semantic\_codes\_design.md}.)

\subsection*{Reasoning against all the pipelines}
Reading the bag-of-codes probe across every run (Fig.~\ref{fig:cviprobe}) reorders what actually
drives downstream quality:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{GMM organization helps} (Exp.~7): A$\to$D $0.29\!\to\!0.37$.
  \item \textbf{The GAN helps the most} --- and this is the surprise: D$\to$D+ jumps
  $0.365\!\to\!0.438$, the single biggest step. Forcing \emph{realistic} reconstructions
  pressures the codebook into more object-coherent, more linearly-separable codes --- a bigger
  semantic lever than either the GMM or CVI.
  \item \textbf{CVI adds nothing on top} (D+$\to$D+CVI $0.438\!\to\!0.437$).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Why CVI was a no-op (and how to fix it).} The most likely cause is a \emph{scale
mismatch}: the reconstruction loss is \texttt{sum}-reduced (hundreds--thousands per image), so a
CVI weight of $0.5$ against a per-image-mean CVI ($\approx\!5$) is negligible --- the aux gradient
barely nudged the codes over $200$ epochs. It is not disproven, just \textbf{under-weighted}: a
scale-matched weight ($\sim$$10^2$--$10^3\times$ larger, or a mean-reduced reconstruction),
stronger augmentations, and the symmetric loss would give it a real chance. As it stands, the
honest ranking is \textbf{GAN $>$ GMM $\gg$ CVI} for downstream, and the best semantic feature
remains the bag-of-codes of a code-prior \emph{GAN} model (D+ / D+CVI, $\approx\!0.44$).

\section{Experiment 10 --- Semantic codes without augmentation: masked code modeling (D+MCM)}
CVI (Exp.~9) belongs to the \emph{invariance} family of self-supervision: all its signal comes from
augmentation, and it needs an anti-collapse crutch (Sinkhorn/me-max) that \emph{fights} the codebook.
When pushed it destabilized the codebook (perplexity drifting down, no plateau) without lifting the
probe. So we switched \emph{families} to \textbf{masked modeling} (the BEiT/iBOT recipe): predict the
code of a \emph{hidden} latch from its visible neighbours. It is augmentation-free, it \emph{uses} the
codebook as its tokenizer instead of policing it, and it has no collapse shortcut --- a constant or
coarse code prediction scores badly, so no Sinkhorn/me-max is needed.

\subsection*{What was added (how it was trained)}
\textbf{D+MCM} is Run~D+ with \emph{one extra auxiliary head}, the reconstruction/GAN path untouched.
Per step: the latent grid $z_{e}$ is formed as usual; a small ViT predictor sees the grid with
$\sim$$50\%$ of the $32\times32$ locations \textbf{masked} (replaced by a learned mask token) and, from
the visible ones, predicts a distribution over the $256$ codes at each masked location. The target is
the model's \emph{own} soft code assignment (the GMM responsibilities) at those locations, detached.
Cross-entropy at the masked locations flows back through the \emph{visible} tokens into the encoder,
pressuring the codes to be \textbf{predictable from context} (= object-part / content structure). The
weight is bounded (a ratio-anchor capped so the effective weight cannot run away --- the lesson from
CVI). Implementation in \texttt{models/masked\_predictor.py} + \texttt{experiments/train\_dualvae.py}.

  $100\%$ active units) --- exactly as a reconstruction-untouching aux head should.
  \item \textbf{It does \emph{not} collapse the codebook.} This is the concrete advantage over CVI: the
  monitored \texttt{MSM Accuracy} (fraction of hidden patches whose code the context predicts) rose
  steadily while codebook perplexity stayed healthy --- there is no ``ignore codes'' shortcut, so the

is not (yet) the large jump the GAN was. Two levers remain to push it: a \emph{stronger} MSM weight now

\texttt{dualvae\_msm\_latent\_D.yaml}) and doubles as a \textbf{native generative prior over the GMM

decode) --- the ``reconstructs at VAE quality, carries a GMM latent, \emph{and} can generate from it''
configuration; its training run is pending.

\section{The overall story}
Putting the ten experiments together gives one coherent picture. The KL weight $\beta$ trades
reconstruction against mixture structure; the code-centered prior escapes that trade-off:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No posterior collapse, ever} (Exp.~1 \& 4): the continuous branch stays
  active and both branches remain load-bearing in all four runs.
  \item \textbf{With the standard prior, $\beta$ forces a rate/separability trade-off:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Low $\beta$ (Run~A):} the continuous branch is free, becomes a
    high-rate near-deterministic code, does the reconstruction, and the discrete codes stay
    tiny $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{best reconstruction} (PSNR $29.1$) but \textbf{no visible
    Gaussian mixture}.
    \item \emph{Higher $\beta$ (Run~B):} the continuous branch is reined in, so the codes
    are forced to spread out and carry the image $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{emerging Gaussian
    mixture} (separability $0.11\!\to\!0.68$) at the cost of reconstruction quality
    (PSNR $29.1\!\to\!27.1$).
  \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The code-centered prior breaks the trade-off} (Exp.~6, Runs~C \& D): tying the
  prior to the codeword gives a \textbf{clean, statistically confirmed mixture at either $\beta$}
  (separability $2.7$/$2.4$, GMM beats a single Gaussian on BIC and likelihood, dip
  $p<0.01$) --- because the mixture comes from the prior \emph{shape}, not the KL strength. Freed
  from needing a strong KL, one can then pick \emph{low} $\beta$ (\textbf{Run~D}) to recover
  near-best reconstruction: $0.870$ SSIM / $28.7$ PSNR, essentially Run~A, \emph{with} a clean
  mixture. \textbf{Run~D is the best-of-both configuration.}
  \item \textbf{Who carries the image} (Exp.~5): in Run~A the codes are mostly a constant
  offset (only $\sim$$36\%$ of their ``help'' is real information); in Runs~B/C/D it is
  $\sim$$98$--$100\%$ genuine per-image information, with the codes carrying the image and the
  continuous branch adding detail.
  \item \textbf{The dual design does not beat a \emph{matched} VAE on reconstruction --- it gets the
  codes for free} (Exp.~8): a complete single-branch ablation at matched capacity+recipe (8-ch,
  LPIPS+GAN) shows removing the discrete branch (a plain VAE) barely moves rFID ($3.97\!\to\!4.08$,
  a tie), while removing the continuous branch (a VQ-VAE) clearly hurts ($3.97\!\to\!5.93$). So the
  continuous branch carries reconstruction and the dual model adds the Gaussian-mixture latent
  \emph{at no reconstruction cost} --- the honest value proposition is a structured, code-organized
  latent at VAE-level fidelity, not a pixel win. Both single-branch baselines still beat RAE.
  \item \textbf{The better organization is measurably useful} (Exp.~7): the GMM makes the
  bag-of-codes a better linear-probe descriptor (accuracy $0.29\!\to\!0.37$ A$\to$D, $+26\%$),
  and the best-reconstruction Run~D is also best downstream on the code features --- though the
  benefit is confined to the discrete codes and to probing (retrieval is flat), and the absolute
  level confirms a reconstruction latent is no substitute for self-supervised pre-training.
  \item \textbf{A perceptual (LPIPS+GAN) objective is a big quality win and the mixture survives}
  (Exp.~8): adding the VQGAN recipe to the code-prior runs cuts \textbf{rFID $\sim$6--8$\times$
  ($32/23\!\to\!3.9/4.0$)} and LPIPS $\sim$4$\times$ ($.17/.12\!\to\!.04$) with PSNR/SSIM flat, and it
  \emph{erases} the $\beta$ gap (C+ $\approx$ D+). The per-code mixture even sharpens
  (separability $\to$$3.0$), though the aggregate marginal smooths toward a single Gaussian
  envelope --- so report FID/LPIPS, not PSNR/SSIM, for these models.
  \item \textbf{The mixture comes from the prior, not the codebook --- a plain VQ-VAE control}
  (Exp.~8): a VQ-VAE with the \emph{same} codebook/GAN but \emph{no} continuous branch or
  code-centered prior reconstructs well ($5.93$ rFID, beating RAE) yet its codes fail the direct
  mixture test --- the codeword-anchored held-out likelihood is \emph{negative} ($-0.97$ vs
  $+0.16$ for D+), i.e.\ its codewords merely $k$-means-tile a single Gaussian. The continuous
  branch is worth $\sim$$2$ rFID points \emph{and} is what turns the codes into genuine mixture
  means (and into a slightly better probe descriptor, $0.406\!\to\!0.438$).
  \item \textbf{A wavelet detail branch did \emph{not} help} (Exp.~8, C++/D++): ported into the
  code-centered-KL DUALVAE it raised rFID ($3.9/4.0\!\to\!4.5/4.4$) and weakened the mixture (D++
  separability $3.0\!\to\!0.83$). The wavelet's gain in \texttt{swd\_dualvae} was tied to its
  band-aware SWD regularizer; the per-code KL mis-shapes the wavelet bands, so the extra
  high-frequency energy reads as artifacts. \textbf{C+/D+ remain the best FID configs.}
  \item \textbf{For downstream semantics, the GAN --- not the GMM or CVI --- is the big lever}
  (Exp.~7 \& 9): bag-of-codes probe accuracy climbs A$\to$D ($0.29\!\to\!0.37$, GMM), jumps
  D$\to$D+ ($0.37\!\to\!0.44$, GAN), and does not move D+$\to$D+CVI ($0.44$). The cross-view
  code-invariance term (D+CVI) preserved reconstruction and the mixture perfectly but added no
  semantic gain --- most likely because its weight ($0.5$) is negligible against the sum-reduced
  reconstruction loss; it needs a scale-matched weight to have a chance.
  \item \textbf{Masked code modeling is the safe way to semantic codes} (Exp.~10): switching from the
  invariance family (CVI) to the masked/generative family (BEiT-style: predict a hidden patch's code
  from its neighbours) removes the collapse shortcut entirely. D+MCM preserved reconstruction
  ($3.97\!\to\!3.85$ rFID) and the mixture, kept the codebook healthy (no collapse, unlike CVI), and
  gave a small monotone gain (bag-of-codes $0.438\!\to\!0.441$, \texttt{z\_vq} $0.357\!\to\!0.361$).
  It is not yet the GAN-sized jump, but it is stable and points at the \emph{generative} sibling
  (masked \emph{latent} GMM modeling), which is also a native prior over the mixture latent.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Recommendation.} For a cleanly separated Gaussian-mixture latent, use the
\textbf{code-centered prior} (\texttt{residual\_continuous} + \texttt{component\_prior} +
\texttt{use\_ema\_codebook}) rather than cranking $\beta$; and since the mixture is
$\beta$-independent, keep $\beta$ \emph{small} (Run~D) to also get the best reconstruction ---
the mixture and the fidelity are no longer in competition. Further tightening of components is
available by lowering \texttt{sigma2\_floor}. When quantifying a branch's contribution, prefer
the \emph{replace-with-mean} intervention over zeroing, to avoid crediting the branch for a
DC/off-manifold artifact.
